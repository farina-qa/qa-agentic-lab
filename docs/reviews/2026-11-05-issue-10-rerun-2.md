# Devil's advocate review: issue #10 (second rerun)

## Earlier findings

1. Two directions per checkbox: resolved, four separate sort criteria.
2. "An image that loads" passes the wrong image: resolved, now "an image of that product that loads".
3. "First opens" has no boundary: resolved, now "When I sign in, the list is sorted by name, A to Z".

## Findings

1. **"I can sort" states the control, not the result** (#10 AC 3 to 6: "I can sort by price, low to high"). A reader can pass this by finding a working option while the list stays unordered. Fix: "the list is ordered by price, low to high" after choosing that option.

2. **Count versus "every product"** (#10 story "see every product"; AC 1 "I see six products"). If the catalogue changes, the want and the criterion disagree and nothing says which wins. Fix: align the want to "the six products", or drop the number from the criterion.

3. **Tie rule is unchecked prose** (#10, line after the criteria: "Products with the same price may appear in either order."). It is not a checkbox, so it is never ticked, and it does not say it applies only to the price sorts. Fix: attach it to AC 5 and 6, or make it its own criterion.

## In its favour

The sign-in default-order criterion fixes the earlier boundary problem: a spec knows which order to expect on arrival, and the four split sort criteria let one failing direction fail alone. I nearly objected to "description" having no pass condition; it is gradable as shown, so I dropped it.

## Verdict

**Objections.** All three earlier findings are resolved. I tried harness bleeding, a prescribed implementation and a broken chain (parent #9, `area:inventory`); none held. I did not re-check the milestone; the last review recorded Phase 2.
